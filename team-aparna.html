Aparna Pushkaran Ajitha

Doctoral Researcher – Epidemiological Modeling, Animal Science

Aparna is a doctoral researcher in the Department of Animal Science at Texas A&M University, working under the guidance of Dr. Karun Kaniyamattam. Her research focuses on resource efficiency in dairy production systems, with a particular emphasis on analyzing the interlinked use of water, energy, and feed in modern dairy farms. By applying systems modeling and decision science approaches, her work aims to improve the sustainability, resilience, and productivity of livestock systems.

Prior to starting her Ph.D., she worked on the development of a system dynamics model for Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD), combining epidemiological modeling and simulation to understand disease transmission pathways and evaluate intervention strategies at the herd and population levels — an experience that fostered her interest in integrating animal health, systems modeling, and sustainability into livestock research.

Projects

System Dynamics Model for the Water-Energy-Food Nexus in Dairy Farms

A whole-herd system dynamics model integrating water and energy modules with an existing dairy herd lifecycle model, enabling stakeholders to optimize water use, energy use, and milk production together.

Agent-Based Model of the Water-Energy-Food Nexus

Represents each cow as an autonomous decision-making agent with individual water, feed, and energy needs, so farm-level resource use emerges bottom-up from thousands of agent interactions — complementing the top-down system dynamics model.

Cost-Benefit Economics of Resource Use Optimization

A web-based decision tool quantifying the return on investment and payback period of water/energy-efficiency technologies (e.g. robotic milking systems) for Texas dairy farms.

Life Cycle Assessment of Texan Dairy Resource Flows

A cradle-to-farm-gate LCA quantifying total water and energy flows — including dry cows, calves, and pasture — to support Dairy Net Zero roadmap reporting and carbon/water footprint credentialing.

System Dynamics Model for Foot-and-Mouth Disease

A system dynamics SEIRC framework simulating Foot-and-Mouth Disease spread and control, linking epidemiological dynamics to on-farm production and resource-use decisions.
